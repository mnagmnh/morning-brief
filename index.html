03 Life Skill of the Day

First aid: the "five-and-five" method for a choking adult

Choking is one of the few emergencies where the right response in the first minute matters enormously, and most people freeze simply because they've never rehearsed the steps. Mayo Clinic's guidance boils it down to something memorable: alternate five back blows with five abdominal thrusts.

If someone is choking but can still cough forcefully or speak, don't intervene — encourage them to keep coughing, since that's the body's own most effective response. If they can't breathe, can't talk, or start turning blue, act: stand slightly behind and to one side, bend them forward at the waist, and strike firmly between the shoulder blades with the heel of your hand — five times. If that doesn't clear the airway, move to abdominal thrusts (the Heimlich manoeuvre): stand behind them, wrap your arms around their waist, make a fist just above the navel, grasp it with your other hand, and thrust sharply inward and upward, five times. Keep alternating five-and-five until the object comes out or the person can breathe again. If you're alone with them, do this first before stopping to call for help; if someone else is nearby, have them call while you act.

Try this today: just say "five and five" out loud to yourself once or twice — that one phrase is what will surface under pressure if you ever need it. Muscle memory beats panic.

07 Uit die Bybel · From Scripture

Filippense 4:6–7 (1933/1953-vertaling)

"Wees oor niks besorg nie, maar laat julle begeertes in alles deur gebed en smeking met danksegging bekend word by God. En die vrede van God, wat alle verstand te bowe gaan, sal julle harte en julle sinne bewaar in Christus Jesus." Filippense 4:6–7, Afrikaans 1933/1953-vertaling

In plain English: Paul doesn't tell his readers not to feel the weight of their circumstances — he tells them what to do with it: turn anxiety into a specific, named request ("in everything, through prayer and petition"), and bring it with thanksgiving already attached, before the outcome is known. The promise that follows isn't that the request gets granted exactly as asked — it's that "the peace of God, which surpasses all understanding" will stand guard over your heart and mind. The Greek word behind "guard" is a military one, the same used for a garrison protecting a city; it's peace as a sentry, not a feeling that simply arrives.

One thing to carry today: pick the one thing you're actually anxious about right now — not a vague list, one specific thing — and turn it into a named request instead of carrying it as unformed worry. Say what you're asking for, out loud or in writing, and attach one thing you're thankful for in the same breath.
